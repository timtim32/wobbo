# Design

## Context

- Every display has one strip window, 360 pt tall (`STRIP_HEIGHT_PT`), anchored to the bottom of the display's usable rect. Content is placed in **walkway points**: x along all strips, y measured from the strip's top. `walkway_to_local(i, p)` turns those into stage pixels of strip `i`; monster views, name labels and the debug drawing all go through it (`overlay_controller.gd`, `walkway_body.gd`, `session_avatar.gd`).
- Input: `RegionRegistry` already has drag capture. A press inside a region sets `_captured_id`, the pass-through target becomes `CAPTURE` (the whole strip under the pointer takes the mouse), and capture ends on release, with a `_process` safety net for a release outside our windows. `region_input` is emitted on **press** only; `AvatarManager._on_region_input` runs "go to session" right away on a left press.
- `SessionAvatar` sets `position` (feet point) from `AvatarMood` every frame, and `position.y = _floor_y()`. The in-progress `dock-dodge` change adds a Dock lift to `_floor_y()`. A `Monster` view's origin is the feet point (body centre x, bottom of the figure), so rotating the view node rotates the buddy around its feet.
- Slots come from the pure `AvatarCast`: `plan()` keeps each shown avatar's `slot`, `slot_center()` maps a slot to x on the home segment.
- The app normally runs at 30 fps (`QuietFps._normal_fps`) and lowers it while quiet.
- Clips are generated by `godot/tools/build_monster_clips.gd` (`body_clips()` dictionary → `body_clips.tres`).

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- A carried buddy that reads as a physical object on a hinge: no lag at the grab point, believable swing, full spins from small circles.
- Physics and gesture logic as pure, unit-tested `RefCounted` helpers, like `dock_watch.gd` and `home_display.gd`.
- Zero cost when nobody is dragging: strips keep their size, no extra windows, nothing new in the frame loop beyond a few checks.

**Non-Goals:**
- A general physics engine; collisions between buddies; rotation while walking or standing.
- Rigid-body accuracy: the buddy is a point mass on a massless rod, tuned to look right.

## Decisions

### D1. Grab gesture: click on release, grab on hold or move
A pure `GrabGesture` (`scripts/core/grab_gesture.gd`) gets `press(point, now)`, `update(point, now) -> StringName` and `release(now) -> StringName`. It returns `&"grab"` once the button has been held for `HOLD_SEC` = 0.25 s or the pointer has moved `MOVE_PT` = 6 pt from the press point, and `&"click"` on a release before that. `AvatarManager` keeps one gesture per press: the left press starts it, `_process` polls `DisplayServer.mouse_get_position()` and `mouse_get_button_state()` (global, so it keeps working when the pointer leaves the strip), and the release ends it. "Go to session" moves from the press to the `&"click"` result. A right press still opens the context menu at once.

To get the release reliably, `OverlayController` emits a new `region_released(id, point)` signal from `_on_click_released` with the captured region. The existing `_process` safety net covers releases outside our windows.

Alternative considered: Godot's built-in drag & drop (`_get_drag_data`) only works for `Control`s inside one window; our buddies are `Node2D` views spread over several windows. Rejected.

### D2. Swing physics: point mass on a moving pivot
A pure `SwingPhysics` (`scripts/core/swing_physics.gd`) holds the state of one carried or falling buddy, in walkway points and seconds, with y pointing down:

- **Grab:** `grab(feet, grab_point, com_offset)`. `com_offset` is the centre of the body relative to the feet (centre of `bounds_pt()`). The rod is `r0 = com - grab_point`; its length `L = max(|r0|, MIN_ROD = 0.3 × body height)` so a grab right at the centre still swings. The angle `θ` is measured from straight down; `θ0 = atan2(r0.x, r0.y)` at the moment of grab, and the body's tilt is `θ - θ0` (0 at grab, so nothing jumps).
- **Carry:** `step(pivot, dt)` with the pivot = the pointer in walkway points. The pivot acceleration `a` is the second difference of a lightly smoothed pivot (critically damped follower, `PIVOT_SMOOTH_SEC` = 0.03) and is clamped to `MAX_ACCEL`. The pivot itself is drawn at the raw pointer, so there is no visible lag. The angle follows the pendulum equation for an accelerating pivot:

  `θ'' = -((g - a.y)·sin θ + a.x·cos θ) / L - DAMPING·θ'`

  with `g` = `GRAVITY_PT` (start 2400 pt/s²) and small `DAMPING` (start 0.6 /s; 1.0 /s after the first tests, so a flicked buddy settles within ~10 s) so the swing dies down over a few seconds but can still build up. `θ` is not wrapped or clamped, so full turns ("hinge") come out of the equation: circling the pointer near the natural frequency `√(g/L)/2π` (about 1 Hz for a Normal buddy) pumps energy in. `|θ'|` is capped at `MAX_SPIN` (3 turns/s) to stay readable.
- **Integration:** fixed sub-steps of 1/240 s (semi-implicit Euler) inside each frame, so it is stable at 30 fps and independent of frame rate.
- **Release:** `release()` switches to free flight. The centre of mass gets the pivot's velocity plus the rotational velocity `θ'·L` perpendicular to the rod; the spin `θ'` continues with `AIR_SPIN_DAMPING`. Gravity pulls the centre of mass down.
- **Landing:** when the feet of an upright body would be below the floor (`floor_y` passed in by the avatar, so the Dock lift is included) while moving down, the body lands: the vertical speed is dropped, the horizontal speed decays over `SLIDE_SEC` = 0.2 s, and the tilt eases to the nearest multiple of 2π over `UPRIGHT_SEC` = 0.15 s. Out of flight, x is clamped to the walkway's ends (bounce with half speed) and the top of the body to y ≥ `-headroom` of the strip below it.
- **Sign:** Godot rotates clockwise on screen for a positive angle (`Vector2.rotated`, `Node2D.rotation`), the opposite of `θ`, so `tilt = θ0 - θ`.
- **Output each frame:** `feet` (walkway points) and `tilt` (radians). Since the view's origin is the feet, `feet = pivot + R(tilt)·(feet0 - grab_point0)` while carried.

Alternative considered: Godot `RigidBody2D` + `PinJoint2D`. It would need a physics world per strip window, mapping between windows, and fighting the kinematic pivot's teleporting; the pendulum is twenty lines and testable headless. Rejected.

### D3. Tilt on the walkway body
`WalkwayBody` gets `tilt := 0.0`. `_update_views()` sets `m.rotation = tilt` on every view (primary and mirrored). `rect_pt()` returns the axis-aligned box of the rotated bounds, so the region, `strips_overlapping()` and the crossing between displays keep working while the buddy spins. Outside a carry tilt stays 0, so nothing else changes.

### D4. Carried state on top of the mood
`SessionAvatar` gets a `_carry` phase: `&""` (normal), `&"held"`, `&"falling"`, `&"landing"`. While not `&""`, `_process` skips the mood's motion (the mood keeps receiving session state, so after landing it continues with what the session asks for), plays `dangle` (held and falling) or `land` (landing), sets `position` and `tilt` from `SwingPhysics`, hides the name labels, and keeps the eye on the pointer. The region is registered every frame as now, so a falling buddy can be caught (a press while `&"falling"` calls `grab` again). After `land` finishes, `_carry` returns to `&""`. `is_going()` avatars refuse a grab (spec: leaving buddy).

### D5. Slot after a drop
New pure `AvatarCast.drop_slot(x, seg_start, seg_width, count) -> int` gives the nearest slot centre. When the landing point lies on the home segment, `AvatarManager` sets the dropped avatar's `slot` to that slot and gives its old slot to the avatar that had the new one (if any). Both are plain `slot` changes: `plan()` keeps whatever slots shown avatars have, and the existing stroll code already walks an avatar back into its slot range without jumping. Landing on another segment leaves the slot as is; the existing `direction_to(home)` check in `_process` then starts `mood.travel()`.

### D6. Headroom: growing the strips while carrying
`OverlayController` gets `begin_headroom()` / `end_headroom()` (reference counted by the avatar manager: begin on grab, end once no buddy is held or falling, after `HEADROOM_LINGER_SEC` = 0.3 s).

- Growing: each strip's window rect becomes its display's usable rect from top to the strip's bottom (`rect_px` extended upwards by `headroom_px[i]`). The window's bottom edge does not move.
- Content does not move: the strip's `stage` gets `position.y = headroom_px` and the `ui` CanvasLayer `offset.y = headroom_px`, so `walkway_to_local()` and every existing caller keep working unchanged, and y < 0 in walkway points now lies in the visible headroom.
- Only window-local conversions inside the controller (`_handle_mouse` → `local_to_walkway`, the pass-through polygon in `_update_passthrough`, `_strip_under_mouse`) add or subtract `headroom_px`. They get a small helper `window_to_walkway(i, px)` / `walkway_to_window(i, p)`.
- While grown, the pass-through stays exactly as today: `CAPTURE` over the whole (grown) window while the button is down, otherwise only the regions. So clicks above the strip still reach the apps behind it while a buddy falls.
- `_update_layout` must not treat the grown rect as a layout change: the strip's base rect stays the source of truth, the grown rect is derived.
- Frame rate: while headroom is active, `QuietFps` uses 60 fps instead of 30 for a smooth swing (`Engine.max_fps`), and returns afterwards.

**Godot / macOS assumptions:** Godot 4.7 `Window.size`/`position` and `DisplayServer.window_set_size/position` resize transparent borderless `NSWindow`s; `MacOverlay.configure_window()` keeps level, collection behaviour and no-shadow after a resize (it is re-applied anyway on the 1 s poll). `DisplayServer.mouse_get_position()` and `mouse_get_button_state()` report the global pointer and buttons even while the pointer is outside our windows.

Alternative considered: a separate full-screen transparent "sky" window per display, shown only during a drag. It avoids resizing the strips but needs a second set of views, stages and coordinate mappings for the carried buddy. Kept as fallback (S1).

### D7. Clips
Add `dangle` (loop, ~0.6 s: alternating leg kicks, arms raised and flailing out of phase, ears flopping, eye wide via the face library) and `land` (one-shot, ~0.35 s: body squash 1.15×0.85 with legs bent, rebound stretch, settle to the `idle` pose) to `body_clips()` in `build_monster_clips.gd`, regenerate the libraries, and add them to the clip tests. `land` ends in the `idle` pose so the one-shot → idle hand-over has no jump.

### Spikes
- **S1 Resizing transparent strip windows on macOS.** Check that growing and shrinking a strip window (root and secondary) in Godot 4.7 has no black or flashing frame, keeps the window level, and keeps the other buddies at the same spot on screen in the same frame. Fallback: the sky window from D6's alternative; second fallback: keep the strips and clamp the pivot to y ≥ top of the strip plus the hanging length (the buddy then stays inside the strip while the pointer goes higher).
  - **Outcome (2026-11-23, built-in display, root strip):** passed, no fallback needed. Growing the root window from 720 to 1898 px tall (to the bottom of the menu bar) with `canvas_transform` offset by the headroom kept the strolling buddy at the same screen position in the same frame, with no black or flashing frame, and a polygon drawn at negative walkway y showed up in the headroom. Two findings for the implementation: (1) after the resize the window level drops from 25 to 3 (Godot re-applies its own always-on-top level), so `MacOverlay.configure_window()` must run after every grow and shrink; (2) a shrink sent as size + position in one frame was once left at the grown size, so the controller re-checks the window rect every frame while it differs from the wanted rect and applies it again. Instead of offsetting `stage` and `ui` separately, the window's `canvas_transform` moves everything on the default canvas, and only the `ui` CanvasLayer needs its own offset.
- **S2 Outcome (2026-11-23):** the user tried picking up, swinging, spinning and dropping in the real app and found the feel right as it is: `GRAVITY_PT` 2400, `DAMPING` 1.0, `MIN_ROD` 0.3, `MAX_ACCEL` 40000, `MAX_SPIN` 3. No further tuning.
- **S3 Outcome (2026-11-23, native build, real drag with `cliclick`):** passed. After a press on a buddy the app became active and stayed active for the whole drag; `DisplayServer.mouse_get_position()` and `mouse_get_button_state()` followed the pointer and held button all the way from the buddy to the middle of the screen, the region capture stayed on, and focus went back to the previous app right after the release. A press sent in the same instant as the pointer arrives on the buddy passes through (the pass-through polygon updates one frame later); a real hover always comes first, so no change.
- **S2 Feel.** Tune `GRAVITY_PT`, `DAMPING`, `MIN_ROD` and `MAX_ACCEL` with the user on a real trackpad and mouse, using a debug scene that shows a single draggable buddy. Acceptance: hanging still within ~3 s after a sideways flick; a full spin after at most ~5 circles of a few cm at ~1 Hz. Fallback if pumping is too hard: lower damping or a slightly shorter effective rod for the dynamics only.
- **S3 Focus during a drag.** macOS activates our app on mouse-down. Check that `_restore_after_click` (deferred after the press) does not break the drag (the window keeps receiving the drag events when it is no longer active). Fallback: postpone the restore until the release while a gesture is pending.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [A grown strip window covers the whole display for a few seconds] → it only grows during a drag, pass-through outside regions is unchanged, and it shrinks back 0.3 s after landing.
- [Clicks become release-based, so "go to session" fires ~0.1 s later than today] → hardly noticeable; a hold-to-grab needs it.
- [Two in-flight changes touch `session_avatar.gd` and the `session-avatars` spec (dock-dodge)] → implement after dock-dodge lands, or rebase on it; landing calls the same `_floor_y()`.
- [Fast pointer moves produce acceleration spikes that make the buddy whip round] → smoothed and clamped acceleration (D2), spin cap.
- [Display layout changes during a drag] → `layout_changed` already remaps x; the swing's own coordinates would be stale, so the carry ends at once and the buddy stands on the floor at its remapped x.
- [A buddy is held when its session ends] → it keeps dangling; leaving starts after it has landed.

## Migration Plan

No data or settings change. Rollback is reverting the change; nothing is persisted.
